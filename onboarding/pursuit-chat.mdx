## Overview

Pursuit Ask AI is the chat interface inside each pursuit that lets you ask questions about that specific solicitation. It reads the pursuit's documents and answers with citations you can click to verify.

**Best for:**
- Evaluating win themes and discriminators
- Analyzing your capabilities against solicitation requirements
- Identifying gaps where subcontractors may be needed
- Extracting requirements and compliance items
- Start building outlines

<Warning>
**Why citations matter:** Every answer includes clickable citations (gray circles). Click them to verify the AI's response against the source document. If the source doesn't support the answer, investigate further before making decisions.
</Warning>

---

## Where to Find It

1. Open **Pursuits** from the left sidebar
2. Click the pursuit you want to analyze
3. Click the **Ask AI** tab in the top bar

## How to Think About Pursuit Ask AI

**This chat is scoped to your pursuit.** When you ask a question, the AI searches the solicitation documents attached to that pursuit. It doesn't know about other pursuits or opportunities.

**Your Library improves outputs.** If you've uploaded past performance, resumes, and capability statements to your Library, you can add them to the chat via **Add from Library** (under the paperclip icon). The AI can then compare the solicitation requirements against your actual content.

**Simple rule of thumb:**
- Ask about *this solicitation* → Pursuit Ask AI
- Ask about *your Library content in general* → General Ask AI (left navigation)

---

## A Few Things to Remember

- Check citations on at least one answer per thread—click the gray circles to verify
- Ask specific questions with constraints: "What are the Section L instructions for the technical volume?" not "Tell me about Section L"
- If an answer feels generic, ask the AI to reference specific sections it used
- Spell out acronyms—use "Performance Work Statement" not "PWS"
- Use **Add from Library** (paperclip icon) before asking capability comparison questions

## Example Use Cases

### Capability Gap Analysis

**When to use:** Identifying where your organization is strong and where you may need subcontractors or teaming partners

**Inputs:** Add past performance and capability files via **Add from Library**

**Prompts:**
> "Compare our capabilities in the Library against the requirements in the SOW. List areas where we fully meet requirements, partially meet requirements, and have gaps"

> "For each capability gap identified, what type of subcontractor or teaming partner would we need to fill it?"

> "Which requirements would be most critical to address through teaming? Rank by evaluation weight"

> "Based on the labor categories required, which roles can we staff internally and which would need subcontractor support?"

---

### Develop Win Themes

**When to use:** Starting proposal strategy and messaging

**Inputs:** Add capability statements and differentiators via **Add from Library**

**Prompts:**
> "Based on the evaluation criteria in Section M and our capabilities in the Library, suggest 3 win themes that connect our strengths to what the government values most"

> "For each suggested win theme, identify the specific solicitation requirement it addresses and the specific Library content that supports it"

> "What proof points from our Library would strengthen each win theme?"

> "What objections might an evaluator have to the theme '[THEME TEXT]'? How would we address them?"

---

### Past Performance Alignment Check

**When to use:** Determining which past contracts to cite

**Inputs:** Add past performance files via **Add from Library**

**Prompts:**
> "What past performance requirements does this solicitation specify? Include contract value thresholds, recency, scope similarity, and number of references required"

> "Which of our past performance files best match these requirements? Explain the alignment for each"

> "Identify any gaps between what the solicitation requires and what our past performance demonstrates"

> "Rank our past performance examples by relevance to this solicitation's evaluation criteria"

---

### Contract Requirements Extraction

**When to use:** Building a compliance matrix or requirements checklist

**Inputs:** None required

**Prompts:**
> "Extract all deliverables from the Statement of Work or Performance Work Statement as a numbered list"

> "List all compliance requirements from Section L including format, page limits, font, and submission instructions"

> "Create a compliance checklist of all certifications, registrations, and clearances required to bid"

> "What are the key performance metrics or SLAs specified in the contract?"

---

### Teaming Strategy Development

**When to use:** Planning your team composition and subcontractor roles

**Inputs:** Add capability statements and past performance via **Add from Library**

**Prompts:**
> "What specialized capabilities does this solicitation require that are outside our core competencies?"

> "Based on the work breakdown in the SOW, what percentage of work could we self-perform vs. subcontract?"

> "What small business subcontracting requirements exist? What categories (8(a), SDVOSB, HUBZone, etc.) are specified?"

> "List the technical certifications and clearances required. Which do we have and which would a subcontractor need to provide?"

---

### Evaluate Win Probability Factors

**When to use:** Understanding what it will take to win

**Inputs:** Add past performance and capability files via **Add from Library**

**Prompts:**
> "What are the key win drivers for this opportunity based on the evaluation criteria? Where would we likely score well and where would we be at risk?"

> "What competitive risks exist? Consider incumbent advantage, mandatory requirements we may not meet, and evaluation factors where we're weaker"

> "Is there an incumbent contractor mentioned? What information is provided about their current performance?"

> "What discriminators could set us apart from competitors based on our Library content?"

---

### Build a Proposal Outline

**When to use:** Starting proposal structure before writing

**Inputs:** None required (Library files help for evidence mapping)

**Prompts:**
> "Based on Section L, create an outline for the [technical/management/past performance] volume with all required sections"

> "Map the outline sections to the evaluation factors in Section M"

> "For each outline section, suggest what evidence from our Library we should reference"

> "Add page limits to each section based on Section L requirements"

---

### Staffing and Key Personnel Analysis

**When to use:** Planning your proposed team and identifying hiring needs

**Inputs:** Add resumes and organizational charts via **Add from Library**

**Prompts:**
> "What key personnel positions does this solicitation require? List qualifications, certifications, and experience requirements for each"

> "Compare the key personnel requirements against the resumes in my Library. Which positions can we fill and which have gaps?"

> "What labor categories are specified in the solicitation? Map them to required qualifications"

> "Are there any personnel continuity or transition requirements we need to plan for?"

---

### Technical Approach Planning

**When to use:** Developing your solution strategy

**Inputs:** Add relevant past performance and technical capabilities via **Add from Library**

**Prompts:**
> "Summarize the technical requirements from the SOW in priority order based on evaluation weights"

> "What specific technical challenges does this solicitation present? How have we addressed similar challenges in our past performance?"

> "What tools, technologies, or methodologies does the solicitation require or prefer?"

> "Based on the SOW, what innovations or efficiencies could we propose that align with our capabilities?"

---

### Pricing Strategy Inputs

**When to use:** Gathering information to support your pricing approach

**Inputs:** None required

**Prompts:**
> "What contract type is specified (FFP, T&M, cost-reimbursement)? What pricing structure does Section L require?"

> "What cost evaluation criteria are in Section M? How is price weighted against technical factors?"

> "Are there any incumbent contract values or government estimates mentioned?"

> "What deliverables or CLINs need to be priced separately?"

## Common Mistakes (and Fast Fixes)

### 1. Asking in the Wrong Chat
**Symptom:** You ask about the solicitation but get answers about your Library, or vice versa

**Why:** You're in General Ask AI instead of Pursuit Ask AI

**Fix:** Go to Pursuits → open the pursuit → click the Ask AI tab

---

### 2. Vague Prompts
**Symptom:** Generic, unhelpful answers

**Why:** The AI needs specific context to give specific answers

**Fix:** Reference specific sections (L, M, C), include constraints, and state what output format you want

---

### 3. Not Checking Citations
**Symptom:** You make a decision based on an AI answer that turns out to be wrong

**Why:** The AI is helpful but not infallible

**Fix:** Click at least 2-3 citations per analysis. Verify source text supports the answer

---

### 4. Expecting Knowledge That Isn't There
**Symptom:** The AI says "I don't have information about..." or gives a vague answer

**Why:** The information isn't in the pursuit documents or your Library

**Fix:** Check what documents are attached to the pursuit. Use **Add from Library** if needed

---

### 5. Over-Trusting Win Probability Answers
**Symptom:** You take "high probability" at face value

**Why:** The AI's assessment is based only on what's in the documents—it doesn't know your competitors or full context

**Fix:** Treat probability assessments as directional. Always verify the reasoning and assumptions

---

### 6. Using Acronyms
**Symptom:** The AI misinterprets your question

**Why:** Acronyms are ambiguous; "CO" could mean many things

**Fix:** Spell out terms: "Contracting Officer" not "CO"

---

### 7. Forgetting to Add Library Files
**Symptom:** The AI can't compare your capabilities to requirements

**Why:** Pursuit Ask AI only sees solicitation docs by default

**Fix:** Use **Add from Library** before asking comparison questions

---

### 8. Asking for the Whole Proposal
**Symptom:** You get a generic, unusable response

**Why:** "Write my proposal" is too broad

**Fix:** Start with the outline. Then ask for one section at a time with specific instructions

---

### 9. Mixing Goals in One Thread
**Symptom:** Thread becomes confusing and answers lose context

**Why:** Too many topics in one conversation

**Fix:** Start a new thread for each goal: one for win themes, one for outline, one for compliance

---

### 10. Skipping AI Reports
**Symptom:** No documentation of your analysis

**Why:** You close the chat without exporting

**Fix:** Generate an AI Report (Word, PDF, or HTML) for any analysis you'll share or reference later
